Pillars

Acquire. Still. First bullet. Repeat.

This lab trains one sequence. The papers below describe related skills. They do not evaluate DTRT Lab.

01 · Move

Acquire the target.

Getting the sight onto the head is a reaching problem. In FPS mouse work, spatial control of the movement predicts acquire time and task performance (Warburton et al., 2023). Train the path in.

Warburton et al., 2023

02 · Stop

Stillness is accuracy.

Among novice rifle shooters, postural balance and hold stability explained about a quarter of score (Mononen et al., 2007). Elite shotgun work shows slower gun motion on better shots (Causer et al., 2010). Analog — not a ranked-play result: a walking shot is a moving platform. UNIT 12 falling only at a stop is what this lab scores, not a paper finding.

Mononen et al., 2007 · Causer et al., 2010

03 · Fire

One committed first bullet.

Quiet eye — the last stable fixation before the act — is longer in elites and on successful shotgun shots (Vickers, 1996; Causer et al., 2010). A lab FPS-like mouse task shows quiet eye too (Dahl et al., 2021). Analog: this lab scores the first committed head at a stop, not the spray after a miss. DTRT does not record gaze.

Vickers, 1996 · Causer et al., 2010 · Dahl et al., 2021

04 · Repeat

Motor acuity. Short dose.

In 7,174 FPS players, hit percentage barely moved while motor acuity — hits per second — rose across days (Listman et al., 2021). About 65% of day-one motor-acuity gain carried overnight, then settled near 40%. Most of that next-day acuity benefit showed up by roughly 30–50 minutes of focused practice. That is a Gridshot dose finding, not a deathmatch trial.

Listman et al., 2021
